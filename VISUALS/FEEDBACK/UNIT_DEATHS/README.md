# FX2 — Soldier crumbling and ground splats

On a lethal hit, the living renderer removes the soldier through its normal sync. The existing death feedback pool captures the final pose and emits five angular pieces by default. Their dimensions follow the compiled unit outline and height. Deterministic variation gives them different launch directions, spin and facet tones within the faction colour. Fragments travel outward, make a small bounce, settle, and fade via ordered coverage. Each piece samples the terrain along its path at emission. Resting bases are centered on the sampled surface, so steep edges can intersect the debris.

Tune **Crumble** and **Crumble Budget** in [UnitVisualConfig.tres](../../../GAMEPLAY/UNITS/UnitVisualConfig.tres). By default, fragments live 0.9 seconds and fade over the last 0.35 seconds. Two shared MultiMesh batches use sparse setters at low counts and preallocated packed buffers at high counts. The configurable defaults allow 256 active detailed deaths, 1,500 active fragments, and 48 new detailed deaths per simulation tick. Each death reserves its whole fragment group under both active limits. Excess deaths skip directly to the independent splat pool, which holds up to 1,024 marks. There is no effect queue.

Each splat is a small, irregular faction-coloured mark. It lasts 3 seconds and fades over the final 1.2 seconds. The Benchmark's **Display and controls → Death effects** toggle clears and disables both crumbling and splats. The shared **Use Unit Fracture** setting disables crumbling and living-unit cracks on scene reload while retaining splats. Benchmark pause freezes both lifetimes. Restarting the battle clears the pools.

For a visual check, watch hurt units turn, then compare Circle Infantry and Dreadnaught deaths on flat ground and a hill. For a stress check, use the same dense battle and camera zoom with **Use Unit Fracture** on and off, and compare frame spikes during mass casualties.
